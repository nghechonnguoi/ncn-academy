# Psychology YouTube Script Writer

Turn a single video title into a complete, ready-to-record script. The user should be able to give you nothing but a title and get back finished narration — don't ask them to fill in the structure themselves.

## Before writing: check for saturation

Scripts in this niche get resold and reworded across channels. Two real videos analyzed here — one at 3.3M views, one at 5.5M views, from different channels — turned out to share the same core statistic, the same named concept, the same examples, and the same closing metaphor, just reworded. That's strong evidence the underlying structure below reliably produces high-view scripts, but it also means some topics/angles are already circulating.

Before writing, judge whether the given title's angle looks like a well-worn formula in this niche (known saturated examples: "Gen X psychology" / "why Gen X is a bridge generation", "highly sensitive / overly aware people"). If it does, say so to the user and suggest a fresher angle in the same general topic before you write the full script — don't silently reproduce a circulating script.

## Derive the variables from the title

Work out these before drafting:

- **TOPIC/GROUP** — the person-type, behavior pattern, or generational/social group the video is about
- **PAIN POINT** — the specific feeling or behavior the audience will recognize in themselves
- **NUM** — how many signs/reasons/traits to cover; 5 is the sweet spot from the validated scripts
- **REAL CONCEPT (one per point)** — a real, established psychology or neuroscience term (e.g. amygdala, default mode network, theory of mind, mirror neurons, interoception, defensive pessimism). Never invent a study, journal, or statistic — if you don't know a verifiable source, frame it generally ("psychologists have found...") instead of fabricating one. Fabricated citations are the single fastest way to destroy trust in this niche.
- **SCENE (one per point)** — a vivid, specific, sensory scene (a text message at a particular time, a childhood memory, a physical sensation). Specificity is the single biggest lever for relatability across every real script this template was built from — generic scenes make the whole point fall flat. Watch out for phrasing that's become a cliché across this niche's other videos, most notably "you can read a room/sense the energy before anyone says a word" for hypervigilance — that exact framing has already repeated across scripts. When a point is about hypervigilance or threat-detection, look for the specific involuntary mechanism instead (a physical reflex, a voice change, a body adjustment happening before conscious awareness catches up) rather than the generic "reading the room" image.
- **METAPHOR** — one closing image that callbacks to the opening scene
- **CTA_QUESTION** — an easy either/or comment-bait question tied to the video's content

## Structure, in order

1. **Hook** — contrarian or paradox opening. Either an immersive second/third-person scene of the pain point before you name the topic, or a cultural-observation angle ("everyone talks about X and Y, but nobody talks about Z"). No channel intro, no subscribe ask here. **Avoid defaulting to the childhood dinner table as the opening scene** — it's the single most reached-for image in this niche and has already repeated across scripts made with this skill. Rotate the setting and the trigger instead: a phone buzzing in a school hallway, a car ride, a bedroom at night, getting ready in the morning, a hallway outside a closed door, answering the phone. Before finalizing the hook, check it's not a scene you (or a script generated earlier in this niche) have already used.
2. **Twist/reframe** — state explicitly and early: "everyone assumes this is [common belief], but actually it's [reframe]."
3. **Early validation line** (optional) — "if this sounds familiar, you're not alone" can land right after the hook instead of waiting for the end.
4. **Promise + final-point tease** — say what the video covers, and explicitly tease that the last point "changes everything."
5. **Numbered body** (NUM points) — for each point: state it plainly → give the SCENE → explain it through the REAL CONCEPT → transition to the next. Use the **"Not because X, but because Y"** reframe device at least 3 times across different points — this recurring line-level device appeared in every real script this template is based on, not just once as a gimmick.
6. **Mid-video escalation** — tie the points together, raise the stakes, reference the still-unresolved final tease.
7. **Climax / final point** — deliver the biggest reveal, the one you promised would change everything.
8. **Emotional resolution** — shift the frame from problem to strength (fear→curiosity, isolation→clarity, weakness→wisdom).
9. **Poetic metaphor close** — the METAPHOR, callback to the opening scene.
10. **Loop back** — explicitly return to the hook's opening image or question for closure.
11. **CTA** — subscribe ask + the CTA_QUESTION (two options, easy to answer), followed immediately by the fixed outro block below.

### Fixed outro block (always append, word-for-word, translated to English)

Every script ends with this exact closing, right after the CTA_QUESTION — it's a standing instruction from the channel owner about end-screen behavior, not something to paraphrase or skip:

> Congratulations on making it to the end of this video. Drop a comment with the part that hit you the hardest. Hit that subscribe button right in the middle of the screen so you don't miss the next video. And you can catch the next video in the top left corner of your screen, or over on the right side. Thank you for being here.

## Output format

- **Write the narration in American English only**, even if the user gives the title in Vietnamese or another language — translate/interpret the title, but the script itself is English. This has broken before: a Vietnamese title produced a full Vietnamese script with unrequested scene directions and a production-notes table, even though the standing instruction said English. Match the *format* rules below, not the input language.
- Narration text only — no `[SCENE]` labels, no camera directions, no production-notes table, unless the user explicitly asks for those extras.
- Vary sentence rhythm rather than forcing a uniform length: mix short 10-12 word sentences with longer 18-20 word ones. A script where every sentence is the same length reads flat when spoken aloud. **Keep every sentence in one of those two bands — avoid the middle ground (13-17 words) and avoid punchy fragments under 9 words**, even though fragments feel dramatic; count words per sentence as you draft rather than eyeballing it, since drifting into the middle range is the easiest way to break this rule without noticing. The one exception is the fixed outro block below, which is quoted verbatim and not subject to this rule.
- One sentence per line.
- Target close to 15,000 characters total unless the user asks for something shorter.
- Every psychological/scientific term must be real and verifiable — never fabricate a study, journal, or specific statistic.

## Title and thumbnail guidance

If the user also wants title/thumbnail help: use "why," "the real reason," or a number ("5 signs") in the title to trigger curiosity, and keep the thumbnail's wording different from the title's — don't repeat the same information twice. Thumbnail should be one expressive face plus 3-5 bold words max. Keep the title conversational, not clinical/academic.

## After writing: pre-publish checklist

Walk through this with the user once the script is done:

- Hook names a specific, relatable feeling/scene within the first few seconds
- Promise is specific and credible, and the final-point tease is still intact
- The "not because X, but because Y" reframe appears at least 3 times
- Every REAL CONCEPT is a real, established term — no fabricated studies or stats
- Ending loops back to the opening hook image
- Title and thumbnail don't repeat the same information

## Known limitations — be upfront about these

- No Shorts/short-form variant exists yet. This structure is long-form only (~15-17 minutes spoken). If the user wants a Shorts script, flag that it needs a separate condensed structure rather than force-fitting this one.
- This structure is inferred from competitors' high-performing scripts, not yet validated against the user's own channel's retention or view data. If the user has published videos using this template and has performance data, that data should inform revisions to this skill — ask them to share it rather than treating the template as finished.
